Privacy Policy for InboxCRM

Effective date: October 29, 2026

InboxCRM ("the Extension", "we", "us", "our") is a browser extension that adds customer-relationship-management tools to WhatsApp Web. This policy explains what information the Extension handles, where it is stored, and the limited circumstances in which any of it leaves your device.

The Extension is published by Extino Ltd.

1. Summary

  • The Extension is local-first. Your CRM data — pipelines, notes, tags, templates, schedules, orders and reports — is stored in Chrome's local extension storage on your own computer.
  • Our servers do not receive your WhatsApp conversations or your CRM records. There is one exception, and it is off until you switch it on: with the optional HTTP API, the messages your own systems ask InboxCRM to send pass through our relay on their way to your browser (section 3.4). The relay does not store them. If you connect an AI app such as Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini to the same relay (this needs the switch Turn on MCP) and switch on reading, what it asks to read (recent chats, a chat's last messages, contacts, orders and your other InboxCRM records) passes through the relay to that app, again without being stored; if you also switch on changes, the changes it asks for travel the same way (section 3.4). If you connect an app by signing in with your InboxCRM account, the relay keeps a record of that connection, never your messages (section 3.11). So that an app can tell you why InboxCRM does not connect, the relay also keeps a few facts about your browser's connections: versions, times and reason codes (section 3.4). With the optional courier updates, a courier's status updates for your parcels wait on the same relay until your browser collects them (section 3.8).
  • Information leaves your device only when you choose a feature that requires it: the optional AI assistant, optional webhooks you configure yourself, the optional HTTP API, courier and cloud-backup features you connect, the optional alerts to your own Telegram or WhatsApp number, the optional Meta Pixel connection to your own Meta ad account, an AI app you connect yourself (and the sign-in for it), and our account and subscription service.
  • We do not sell your data. We do not use it for advertising. If you switch on the optional Meta Pixel connection, your browser sends the events described in section 3.10 to your own Meta ad account; we do not receive them. We run no analytics or usage tracking inside the Extension. The only telemetry is automatic error reports (see section 3.5), which never contain message content, contact names or phone numbers.

2. Information the Extension handles

2.1 Data stored locally on your device

Stored in chrome.storage.local and never transmitted by us:

  • Contact names and phone numbers (WhatsApp chat identifiers) attached to the CRM records you create
  • Kanban boards, stages, deal values, tags, categories and custom tabs
  • Notes you write about contacts
  • Quick-reply templates and any images, video, audio or documents you attach to them
  • Scheduled messages, appointments, follow-up rules, chatbot flows and auto-reply rules you configure
  • Order and delivery records, and any custom contact profile fields you add
  • Your product list and stock levels, your income and expense entries, and the business profile you set up (country, currency, business type and which features are switched on)
  • Campaign recipient lists and send logs
  • Sending-safety settings and the counters that enforce your daily and hourly limits
  • Interface preferences (language, theme, privacy blur)
  • Backups you export or schedule

2.2 WhatsApp message content

To provide its features, the Extension reads content from the WhatsApp Web page you have open — the currently selected conversation, its participants, and recent message text. This is used to display the panel, to evaluate the automation rules you set up, and, if you use the AI assistant, to give the AI model context. Unless you turn on Chat backup (described at the end of this policy), the Extension does not keep a copy of your chat history; only the CRM records you create are stored.

2.3 Credentials

  • Any AI provider API key you choose to save
  • The HTTP API token the Extension generates when you first switch on the HTTP API, Turn on MCP or courier updates (all three use the same token)
  • Your Extino session token and cached subscription status
  • Courier account details you choose to connect: your Koombiyo API key and its API password, or your Royal Express merchant e-mail and password, with the sign-in tokens they return
  • The key of a Telegram bot, if you connect one for alerts (section 3.9)
  • The secret part of your courier-updates address (section 3.8)
  • The Pixel ID and access token of your Meta Pixel, if you connect one (section 3.10)

All are stored locally. API keys and courier credentials are sent only to the provider they belong to; they, the Telegram bot key, the Meta access token and the courier-updates address are left out of the backup files the Extension creates. The HTTP API token and your session token are presented to our relay while the HTTP API, Turn on MCP or courier updates is switched on (sections 3.4 and 3.8). The Telegram bot key is given to our relay only if you switch on Telegram alerts for the HTTP API (section 3.4), and only for as long as they are on.

3. When information leaves your device

3.1 AI assistant (optional)

If you use the AI assistant to draft or improve a reply, the Extension sends a prompt to the AI provider you selected. That prompt contains message text from the conversation you have open (by default the most recent 15 messages), the contact or group name, and anything you typed in the composer.

Depending on your settings the recipient is one of:

  • OpenRouter — openrouter.ai
  • OpenAI — api.openai.com
  • Google Gemini — generativelanguage.googleapis.com
  • Groq — api.groq.com

Each provider processes that data under its own privacy policy and terms. We do not receive a copy. If you never use an AI feature, no message content leaves your browser.

If you enable per-message AI rewording in bulk campaigns, the message text for each recipient is likewise sent to the provider you selected.

3.2 Account and subscription (api.inboxcrm.extino.net)

The Extension communicates with our account service at https://api.inboxcrm.extino.net to sign you in, read your profile, check your subscription status (re-checked in the background about once an hour), retrieve pricing, and open Stripe checkout or billing-portal sessions.

Data handled for this purpose may include your user ID, email address, display name, profile image, session token and subscription state. No WhatsApp message content and no CRM record is ever sent to this service.

Payment card details are collected by Stripe on Extino-hosted billing pages. They never pass through the Extension, and we do not store them.

Since version 2.1.3 the Extension uses an account service dedicated to InboxCRM at api.inboxcrm.extino.net, backed by its own Firebase project (inboxcrm-5d961, Google) that holds only InboxCRM accounts and plans. Payments are still processed by Stripe through the same Extino Stripe account. The service runs on Cloudflare Workers (Cloudflare, Inc.).

3.3 Webhooks you configure

The Extension can post event notifications to web addresses you enter in its Webhooks screen. Every event is off until you switch it on and give it an address, which must start with https://. The events are: a new incoming message, a customer arriving from a Facebook or Instagram ad, a quick reply sent, an automation reply sent, a follow-up sent, a scheduled message sent, a pipeline change, a WhatsApp label change, a chat marked as done, a courier update for a parcel, an order created and an order changed.

Every event carries the WhatsApp chat identifier it is about (which contains the contact's phone number) and the event's own details: for a new message, its text, type and time; for the others, things such as the rule, stage or label involved. The two order events carry the whole order: its number, status, lines and amounts, the payment state and method, the courier and tracking number, your note on the order, and the customer's name, phone number and delivery address as written on the order. If you switch the fields on, it also carries the contact's name, the contact profile you filled in, your own WhatsApp number and, for the new-message event, the picture or file in the message (up to about 340 KB). The Extension sends each event straight from your browser to your address and keeps a list of the last 50 deliveries per event on your device. You choose the destination and are responsible for its security and for whatever lawful basis you need to send that data there. We never receive these events.

3.4 HTTP API and its relay (optional)

The HTTP API lets your own systems (your website, your shop software, an automation tool) send a WhatsApp message through your browser with an HTTPS request. It is off until you switch it on in Settings. A browser cannot be reached from the internet directly, so the request travels through a relay we operate at bridge.extino.net (with inboxcrm-bridge.extino.workers.dev as a second address), which runs on Cloudflare Workers, a service of Cloudflare, Inc.

While the HTTP API is on:

  • The Extension keeps a connection open to the relay. To open it, the Extension presents the HTTP API token it generated for you and your Extino session token, which the relay may use to confirm that your plan is active.
  • When one of your systems posts a request to the relay with your token, the relay passes it to your browser: the recipient's phone number, the message text, and any picture or file the request contains or points to (the relay downloads a file from the address your system gave it). The Extension then sends the message through WhatsApp Web, within your own sending limits, and returns the result (a message identifier or an error) the same way.
  • The relay holds a request in memory only, for as long as the delivery takes: at most a couple of minutes. It does not write phone numbers, message text or files to storage, and its logs contain only the kind of request, the result code and an error code. On disk it keeps a list of the times of your requests in the last minute, which enforces the limit on requests per minute. Since October 28, 2026 it also keeps which InboxCRM account (your user id, not your e-mail address) your token belongs to, and, for that account, which of its last 3 connections to the relay is open, so that the AI apps you connect by signing in reach your browser (section 3.11).
  • Connection records for troubleshooting, since October 29, 2026, so that an AI app you connect can tell you why InboxCRM does not connect (below). For every connection of your browser, whichever switch opened it (the HTTP API, Turn on MCP or courier updates), the relay keeps with each of those last 3 connections the Extension version it reported, whether that version has the Turn on MCP switch, and why it last disconnected when the Extension said so (everything that kept it open was switched off, you signed out, or the plan is not active). For your account it keeps the time and reason of the newest connection it turned away (no active plan, or a token of another InboxCRM account), and for your token the same (no valid sign-in, no active plan, or a token of another InboxCRM account). The same reason is written at most once every 10 minutes, and each record is replaced by a newer one. These records hold times, version numbers and reason codes only: no phone number, message, name, e-mail address or token.
  • The relay never receives your incoming messages, your chats or your CRM records, unless you switch on reading or changes for an AI app (below). Otherwise it only sees what your own systems send to it.

Use with Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and other AI apps (MCP), from version 2.2. The same relay also answers AI apps that support the Model Context Protocol, such as Anthropic's Claude (on the web, on the desktop and on mobile) and Claude Code, OpenAI's ChatGPT, or Google's Gemini and Gemini CLI. Three switches in the Extension's MCP window (on WhatsApp Web, the MCP button in the InboxCRM rail; or the MCP card on the Settings page) decide what an app may do. The first, Turn on MCP, is MCP's own switch. On a new install it is off until you switch it on; if you update from version 2.1 with the HTTP API switched on, it starts on, so that a tool that already uses your token keeps working, and the other two switches start off. While it is on, the Extension keeps the connection to the relay open as described above, presenting the same HTTP API token and session token, also while the HTTP API is off. While it is off, the Extension refuses every request from an AI app except the connection check described below. An app connects in one of two ways: at the address https://bridge.extino.net/mcp, after you sign in there with your InboxCRM account (section 3.11), which needs only Turn on MCP; or at an address that contains your token, which works only while both Turn on MCP and the HTTP API are switched on, so switching the HTTP API off stops everyone who has the token. Either way the requests reach your browser through the relay as described here, and nothing happens until you add the address to an app yourself. Through it an app can send messages and start workflows exactly as the HTTP API can.

Whatever the switches say, a connected app can check the connection and ask why InboxCRM does not connect. For that, your browser (when it is connected) answers with yes or no whether WhatsApp Web is ready, whether you are signed in with an active plan and which of the three switches and the HTTP API are on, together with the Extension's version and your local time and time zone; the relay adds the connection records described above for your account (or, at a token address, for that token). These answers contain no message, name, phone number or e-mail address, and nothing is stored when an app asks.

The second switch, Let Claude read my chats and orders, is off until you switch it on. While it is on, a connected app can also ask for your recent chats (names, numbers, unread counts and a preview of the last message), the last text messages of one chat (at most 50), a search of your CRM contacts, a list of your orders, and the other records you keep in InboxCRM (quick replies, Automations, workflows, follow-ups, schedules, campaigns and recipient lists, the pipeline, contacts and notes, labels, reminders, orders, stock, expenses, the settings of Call a person and of your webhooks — webhook addresses are shown without anything after the host name, because they often contain a key — and your reports). Your browser sends each answer back through the relay to the app. The relay passes it on in memory and does not store or log it; what the app does with it afterwards is governed by that app's own terms: for Claude those of Anthropic, for ChatGPT those of OpenAI, for Gemini those of Google.

The third switch, Let Claude change my InboxCRM (create, edit, delete), is also off until you switch it on. While it is on, a connected app can ask your browser to create, change or delete those records, to send a quick reply, an order document or a campaign (always within your sending limits and quiet hours), and to book a courier for an order with your own courier account. The changes are made in your browser, by the same code as the pages, and stay in your browser's storage; nothing extra leaves your device because of this switch, except the answers to the app described above and the files it asks to store (a picture for a quick reply, for example), which the relay downloads for it or holds in memory for at most two minutes until your browser has fetched them. Deletes and edits that remove parts, bulk sends, status posts, courier bookings, a new automation without a trigger (which would answer every message) and changes of where your data or alerts go (a webhook's address, or the numbers, routes and words of Call a person) are only carried out on a second request after the app was shown what would happen. Your browser keeps a list of the last 100 changes made this way (What Claude changed), which stays on your computer and is in no backup. Credentials — courier passwords and keys, your Telegram bot key, your Meta Pixel token, your HTTP API token, AI keys — backups, your plan and sign-in, the HTTP API's switch and these three switches can never be read or changed this way. None of the three switches is ever restored from a backup.

Alerts when your computer is off, from version 2.2. A request can only be sent while your browser is connected. Two further options, each off until you switch it on, let the relay tell you when a request could not be delivered for that reason:

  • Send me a Telegram alert. The Extension gives the relay the key of the Telegram bot you connected in Call a person (section 3.9), the id of its chat and your time zone. The relay keeps them in its storage until you switch the alert off, switch the HTTP API off or make a new token, and uses them only to send that chat a message through Telegram's Bot API: how many requests failed, of which kind (text, picture, file, workflow), when, and the last four digits of the last phone number. Never the message text. At most one such message is sent every 30 minutes; meanwhile the relay keeps those counts, times and the masked number until it has told you.
  • Failure web address. The relay keeps the address you type and posts to it, for each request that failed this way, the error, the kind of request, the phone number your own system gave and the time. Never the message text or files.

The relay's logs do not contain the bot key, the chat, the address or any phone number.

The token works like a password: anyone who has it can send messages from your WhatsApp number. You can replace it or switch the HTTP API off at any time in Settings; requests with the token stop at once, and the connection closes unless Turn on MCP or courier updates keep it open for their own use. Under Advanced you can point the Extension at a relay you run yourself instead of ours; in that case nothing passes through our relay.

3.5 Error reports

To find and fix bugs, the Extension sends an automatic report to our error monitoring provider, Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.), when it crashes or when a WhatsApp Web change breaks one of its features. A report contains the error message and stack trace, the Extension version, your browser version and operating system, and which part of the Extension failed. Before a report is sent, the Extension removes message text, contact names, phone numbers, chat identifiers, e-mail addresses and session tokens; long digit sequences and page addresses are redacted as well. Reports are not linked to your Extino account and are deleted by Sentry after 90 days. Error reporting is switched off in builds where no reporting key is configured.

3.6 Courier integrations (optional, Sri Lanka)

If you connect a courier account in the Order manager and press Send to Koombiyo or Send to Royal Express on an order, the Extension sends that order's delivery details to the courier so they can collect and deliver the parcel: the customer's name, phone number and delivery address (town and district), your order number, a short description of the package, its value, the amount to collect on delivery, and the courier waybill number. To do this it signs in to the courier with the account details you saved. It also queries the courier for the parcel's tracking status, shipping labels and, if you ask, the deletion of a parcel. Koombiyo Delivery is operated by Koombiyo Delivery (Pvt) Ltd; Royal Express is reached through its delivery-management platform, Curfox. Nothing is sent to a courier until you connect the account and press the send button on a specific order, and the courier options only appear when your business country is Sri Lanka.

3.7 Address data for your country

To recognise addresses in chats, the Extension downloads a public list of regions and towns for the country you chose during setup from api.inboxcrm.extino.net (for example /geo/lk.json). The request contains only the country code; no chat content or personal data is sent. The list is cached on your device for seven days. For Sri Lanka a copy is built into the Extension, so it works without this download.

3.8 Courier updates (optional)

If you switch on Courier updates in the Order manager, the Extension shows you an address on our relay (bridge.extino.net/hook/…) that you give to your courier. When the courier reports that a parcel moved, it sends the update to that address, and the Extension uses it to update the matching order on your device.

  • The relay stores each update as the courier sent it — typically the waybill number, the new status, the date and the delivery charge — until your browser collects it, and for at most 7 days. It is then deleted. At most 500 updates are held for one address.
  • The relay does not read or use the content of an update, and its logs contain only the kind of request and the result code.
  • The address contains a secret. Anyone who has the address can send updates to your orders, but cannot read anything and cannot send a message. You can replace the address or switch courier updates off at any time; switching off deletes anything still waiting.
  • To keep the connection to the relay open, the Extension presents your Extino session token, as for the HTTP API (section 3.4), and the relay notes which InboxCRM account the token belongs to and keeps the connection records for troubleshooting, as described there. The HTTP API itself does not have to be switched on.

3.9 Alerts when a customer needs a person (optional)

Call a person is off until you switch it on. It tells you when an automated reply cannot continue: for example the customer asks for a person, or a message could not be sent. An alert contains the customer's name, phone number and last message, and goes only where you choose:

  • Telegram. The Extension sends the alert from your browser straight to Telegram's Bot API (api.telegram.org), using the key of a bot you created yourself, to the Telegram chat you connected. Our servers are not involved. Telegram processes it under its own policy. (The separate HTTP API alerts of section 3.4 do use our relay, and only if you switch them on.)
  • WhatsApp. The Extension sends the alert as a WhatsApp message from your own WhatsApp account to the phone number you typed.
  • Sound. A chime and a desktop notification on your own computer. Nothing leaves your device.

3.10 Meta Pixel (optional)

Meta Pixel is off until you enter the Pixel ID and access token of your own Meta Pixel and switch it on. It is for businesses that advertise on Facebook or Instagram and want Meta to know which of their WhatsApp chats became sales. While it is on, the Extension sends two kinds of event from your browser straight to Meta's Conversions API (graph.facebook.com), using your token:

  • Lead — when a customer who came from one of your Facebook or Instagram ads writes to you. Sent once per customer and ad. You can also send a Lead yourself with Send Lead in that customer's chat (the Orders tab of the chat tools); that is sent at most once per chat, and not when a Lead for that customer was already sent.
  • Purchase — when an order reaches the point you chose (confirmed, paid or delivered), or when you press Send Purchase now on the order in its chat. Sent once per order either way.

An event you send with one of these buttons carries the same data as one the Extension sends by itself, described below, with the time you pressed the button. The buttons are only offered while that event is switched on, and group chats send nothing.

Each event contains the time and a SHA-256 hash of the customer's phone number, computed in your browser; the number itself is not sent. A Purchase also contains the order's value, currency, order number and number of items. Names, message text, addresses and the items bought are not sent. Meta uses the hash to recognise the customer's Facebook or Instagram account and uses the events for the measurement and delivery of your advertising, under its own terms and policies.

  • Our servers are not involved and we never receive these events.
  • An event that could not be delivered waits on your device for up to 7 days and is then dropped. A list of the last 30 results is kept on your device; it contains no token and no hash.
  • You decide to share these events with Meta, and you are responsible for telling your customers and for whatever lawful basis or consent that requires where you and they are.
  • Switching Meta Pixel off, or removing the token, stops the events at once. A Purchase already sent cannot be withdrawn by the Extension.

3.11 Connecting an AI app by signing in (optional, from version 2.2)

You can connect Claude, Claude Code, ChatGPT, Gemini, Gemini CLI or another app that supports the Model Context Protocol to the relay's address https://bridge.extino.net/mcp by signing in with your InboxCRM account, without copying a token. Nothing described here happens until you add that address to an app yourself and start its sign-in.

  • Sign-in. The app opens a sign-in page that our relay serves at bridge.extino.net. The page signs you in with Firebase Authentication (Google), in the Firebase project that holds InboxCRM accounts (inboxcrm-5d961, section 3.2): with your Google account, or with your e-mail address and password, which go to Firebase and not to our relay. The page then hands the relay a short-lived proof of the sign-in from Firebase (an ID token). The relay checks it and does not keep it. Only an account with a verified e-mail address can connect an app; if yours is not verified yet, the page asks Firebase to send you a verification e-mail. While a sign-in is in progress, a cookie that holds a random value ties it to your browser. A sign-in must be finished within 15 minutes, and nothing is stored for it before you press Allow.
  • Consent. A second page names the app, the account you signed in with, the address your browser goes back to and what the app will be able to do. Allow gives the app its own access token, valid for 1 hour, and a refresh token, valid for 60 days and replaced each time it is used. Cancel connects nothing.
  • What the relay stores, per InboxCRM account (your user id): for each connected app, its name and identifier, the host its sign-in returns to (for example claude.ai or chatgpt.com), the scope, when it was connected and last used, and SHA-256 hashes of its tokens and of the single-use sign-in codes; and the last 3 connections of your InboxCRM to the relay (the ids of their records on the relay, not your token), with when each connected and whether it is still open, with the connection records for troubleshooting of section 3.4. For each HTTP API token it stores the account the token belongs to. It also keeps the apps that registered themselves with it and the descriptions that apps publish about themselves (names and return addresses), which contain nothing about you.
  • What it does not store: your e-mail address, your password, the Firebase ID token, a token or code in readable form, your IP address, or any message, chat, contact or order. Requests from a connected app are handled as described in section 3.4: what they read or change passes through in memory only, within your switches.
  • How long. A connected app stays connected until you disconnect it, or until it has not renewed its sign-in for 60 days; its record is then deleted the next time the relay handles a sign-in for your account or InboxCRM shows your connected apps. A sign-in code expires after 5 minutes. The hashes of used refresh tokens are kept until those tokens would have expired, so that a stolen one is recognised. At most 20 apps are kept per account; a new one replaces the one used least recently. The list of connections holds the last 3 and changes as your browsers connect; the version and the reason kept with a connection go with it. The newest turned-away connection is kept until a newer one replaces it. The link between a token and your account, and the connection records, are not removed when you make a new token or switch the HTTP API off; you can ask us to delete them (section 11).
  • Your control. The MCP window in InboxCRM (on WhatsApp Web, the MCP button in the rail; or the MCP card on the Settings page) lists the apps connected to your account under Connected apps. Disconnect deletes an app's connection, and its access ends at once. The three switches of section 3.4 decide whether any connected app may reach InboxCRM and what it may read or change.
  • The demo account. For the reviewers of app directories (such as Anthropic's directory of Claude connectors and OpenAI's ChatGPT app directory), the relay has a demo account, review@inboxcrm.net. Signing in with it reaches a sample shop with made-up data that runs on our relay, never a browser or a WhatsApp account, and nothing done there is sent anywhere. Each connected app gets its own copy, which goes back to the sample every 24 hours; a copy that nobody uses for 3 days is emptied.

4. How information is used

Information handled by the Extension is used only to:

  • Display and organise your WhatsApp conversations inside the CRM
  • Store the notes, tags, templates, pipelines and records you create
  • Send the messages you compose, schedule or approve
  • Apply the sending-safety limits you configure
  • Generate AI drafts when you request them
  • Deliver the webhook events you subscribed to
  • Send Lead and Purchase events to your own Meta Pixel, if you switched that on
  • Pass on the messages your own systems send through the HTTP API
  • Sign you in to the AI apps you connect, pass their requests to your browser, and tell them why InboxCRM does not connect when it does not
  • Verify your subscription
  • Diagnose crashes and broken features from anonymised error reports

We do not use it for advertising, profiling, credit decisions, or sale to any third party. The Meta Pixel connection (section 3.10) serves your own advertising account: your browser sends those events to Meta on your instruction, and they never reach us.

6. Retention and deletion

  • Local data stays on your device until you delete it. You can remove individual records inside the Extension, export an encrypted backup, clear the Extension's storage from chrome://extensions, or uninstall the Extension, which removes its local storage entirely.
  • Back up first if you want to keep your records. Use the Extension's Backup screen to export before uninstalling; the exported file is yours to keep or delete.
  • AI providers retain prompts according to their own policies. Review the policy of whichever provider you configure.
  • The HTTP API relay keeps no message, phone number or file after a request is delivered or fails. The per-minute request times it stores are replaced as new requests arrive. If you switch on alerts for when your computer is off, it keeps your Telegram bot key, chat id, time zone and failure web address until you switch them off, switch the HTTP API off or make a new token; the counts and masked number of failures waiting for the next Telegram alert are deleted once that alert is sent. What an AI app reads through the relay is never stored. The InboxCRM account a token belongs to is kept with that token's record on the relay; you can ask us to delete it.
  • Connected AI apps (section 3.11): the record of an app stays on the relay until you disconnect it, or until the app goes 60 days without renewing its sign-in. Hashes of used refresh tokens are kept until those tokens would have expired, at most 60 days. The relay keeps the last 3 connections of your InboxCRM and replaces older ones as new ones come. A demo copy for reviewers goes back to its sample every 24 hours and is emptied after 3 days without use.
  • Connection records for troubleshooting (section 3.4): the version and the disconnect reason kept with one of your last 3 connections go when that connection drops out of the 3; a disconnect reason is also dropped when that browser connects again. The newest turned-away connection, per account and per token, stays until a newer one replaces it. They are not deleted otherwise; you can ask us to delete them (section 11). Nothing is stored when an AI app asks why InboxCRM does not connect.
  • Webhook deliveries: the list of recent deliveries is stored on your device and removed with the event or with the Extension. What the receiving system keeps is up to its operator.
  • Meta Pixel events: the list of recent results and any event still waiting are stored on your device and removed with the Extension. What Meta keeps is governed by its own terms.
  • Account records may be retained for account, billing, fraud-prevention, legal and support purposes. To request access to or deletion of that account data, contact extinoltd@gmail.com.

7. Security

We apply reasonable technical and organisational safeguards. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure. Practical steps you should take:

  • Keep your AI provider API key private, and prefer a key with a spend limit.
  • Only send webhooks to endpoints you control and trust.
  • Treat your HTTP API token like a password. Give it only to systems you trust, and make a new one in Settings if it may have leaked.
  • Treat your Meta access token like a password. If it may have leaked, make a new one in Meta Events Manager and remove the old one.
  • On the InboxCRM page that asks you to allow an AI app, press Allow only if you started the connection yourself just now, and disconnect apps you no longer use.
  • Store exported backup files somewhere safe; they contain your CRM data.

Chat backup (local archive and optional cloud copy)

InboxCRM can keep a local archive of your WhatsApp chats, contacts and media in your browser's extension storage on your own computer, and export it as a ZIP file. On a new install it is off: nothing is archived until you switch on automatic backup or press Back up now on the Chat backup page. This archive never leaves your device unless you turn on Cloud backup.

If you connect a WA Backup cloud account, an encrypted copy of the archive is uploaded to a server operated by Extino Ltd. The copy is encrypted on your device with AES-256-GCM using a recovery key that is generated on your device and never sent to us; the server stores only ciphertext, opaque record references and timestamps, and cannot read your messages. You can disconnect the account at any time, and the account settings in the web viewer let you delete every stored record. If you lose your recovery key, the cloud copy cannot be decrypted by anyone, including us.
